Strategic options memo

Attacks in the Red Sea are likely to resume. Halvorsen should cover the parts gap now.

For the COO. Wednesday 1 April 2026, looking ahead to 1 October.

Bottom line

  1. It is about even odds (46%, give or take 6) that Houthi attacks on merchant ships resume at scale by 1 July, up from 31% a week ago.

    Moderate confidence

  2. The big carriers will likely stay on the Cape route through the summer whatever happens (64%), so the Red Sea won't reopen soon even in the good case.

    High confidence

  3. If attacks resume, Gebze runs out of compressors around 26 May. That one gap is more than half of the $14.2M expected cost to us by October.

    High confidence

  4. We recommend option C: five moves costing $2.4M that protect $11.9M. Decide by Friday 3 April, before Cape space for May sells out.

    Recommendation

1What changed this week

For two months the chance of renewed attacks crept up a point or two a week. Last week it jumped 15 points. Three things moved it:

  • Pushes it up Iranian missile parts bound for Yemen were intercepted twice in March, which says the Houthis are restocking.
  • Pushes it up Houthi statements on 26 and 29 March named commercial shipping as a target again, for the first time since October.
  • Pushes it up Lloyd's insurers widened the Red Sea listed area on 24 March. They tend to move a few weeks before attacks do.
  • Holds it down Gaza truce talks restart on 7 April. A signed truce would take away the Houthis' stated reason to attack (18% by the end of June).

Totality reached 46% by splitting the question into smaller ones it can check against the record, such as how many attack advisories the UK's maritime office posts in a month. It declined the parts it has no skill at this far out. See how it got there.

2What it means for Halvorsen

Most of our exposure is one lane: compressors from Busan and control boards from Kaohsiung into Gebze, about 38 containers a week. Carriers that went back to Suez in February would turn round again, and the Cape adds 12 days. Gebze holds 18 days of compressors, so the pipeline runs dry around 26 May and the lines stop.

3Three ways to respond

A. Watch and wait

Keep everything as it is and act if a tripwire trips. Costs nothing now, but by the time attacks are confirmed, Cape space and air freight cost about twice as much.

$0 nowprotects $4.1M

B. Cover the parts gap

Logistics books Cape space, procurement lifts compressor stock and Gulf key accounts serve Saudi orders from Dammam. Leaves insurance and systems exposed.

$2.1Mprotects $10.2M

C. Cover the gap, and lock in costs Recommended

Everything in B, plus treasury locks war-risk cover and freight rates and IT tests the backup route for Gulf systems. The extra $0.25M buys $1.7M of protection.

5What would change our minds

If the UK maritime office logs four or more attacks in any two weeks
attacks are under way: fly all control boards and extend Cape bookings to twelve weeks.
If a Gaza truce is signed and holds for two weeks
the chance falls toward 25%: release the second half of the Cape bookings.
If war-risk premiums pass 1% of hull value
move Gulf deliveries overland from Dammam to Jeddah instead of by sea.

6Outside your watch list

Totality watches for risks no one asked about and flags them when their odds move. Three are worth a look this week:

  • 21%Undersea cables cut in the Red Sea, up from 12%. Ships are dragging anchors near Hodeidah and repair ships can't get insurance to work there. Our Gulf order systems run over these cables.
  • 9%Suez Canal pilots strike, up from 4% after pay talks broke down on 27 March. A stoppage would trap southbound Gulf shipments.
  • 6%Sea mines found in the shipping lanes. Unlikely, and not moving much, but it would shut the route outright for weeks: a gray swan.

7What we don't know

Totality split the question into 2,561 smaller ones and declined 462 of them, because nothing in the record lets it forecast them well six months out.
