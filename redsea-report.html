Attacks in the Red Sea Are Likely to Resume

Halvorsen should cover the parts gap now.

Wednesday 1 April 2026, looking ahead to 1 October. Forecasts by the Totality engine.

Bottom Line

  1. It is about even odds (46%, give or take 6) that Houthi attacks on merchant ships resume at scale by 1 July, up from 31% a week ago.

    Moderate confidence

  2. The big carriers will likely stay on the Cape route through the summer whatever happens (64%), so the Red Sea won't reopen soon even in the good case.

    High confidence

  3. If attacks resume in early May, Gebze runs out of compressors around 26 May. Its parts lane is more than half of the $14.2M we expect to lose by October.

    High confidence

  4. Our recommendation: five moves costing $2.4M that cut the expected loss to $4.7M and the bad case from $61M to $28M. Decide by Friday 3 April, before Cape space for May sells out.

    Recommendation

1What Changed This Week

For two months the chance of renewed attacks crept up a point or two a week. Last week it jumped 15 points. Totality traces the jump to four signals, three pushing it up and one holding it down.

It reached 46% by splitting the question into smaller ones it can check against the record, such as how many attack advisories the UK's maritime office posts in a month, and declined the parts it has no skill at this far out.

2What It Means for Halvorsen

Most of our exposure is one lane: compressors from Busan and control boards from Kaohsiung into Gebze, about 38 containers a week. Carriers that went back to Suez in February would turn round again. The Cape adds 12 days at sea, and the schedule chaos of a reroute adds more. Gebze holds 18 days of compressors.
