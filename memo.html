Prepare for New Sanctions

A closer look at whether Washington widens its Iran sanctions this fall: the key stakeholders, four possible futures, how each scenario would hit Halvorsen, and the moves that pay off in all cases.

1Bottom Line

New sanctions are a little less likely than not. If they’re imposed, we’d have about 30 days to stop dealing with two resin suppliers, three Dubai traders, and two feeder ships. Re-sourcing Gebze’s resin and lining up a second bank pay off either way, so we can start both this month and hold off on the costlier steps for now.

  1. Moderate confidence

    The Administration is more likely to widen today’s Iran sanctions than to write new ones, so buyers and shippers of Iranian petrochemicals would be hit first.

  2. High confidence

    Resin is our biggest exposure. Two Gulf suppliers that take feedstock from an Iranian-linked joint venture carry 38% of Gebze’s resin.

  3. Low confidence

    Any wind-down window is likely to be 30 days or shorter. New resin takes 6 to 8 weeks to qualify, so waiting for the announcement would be too late.

2Key Stakeholders

The players whose choices set the odds.

  • Pushing the odds up

    Office of Foreign Assets Control

    Wants to show “maximum pressure” is working, and can widen today’s sanctions without Congress.

    Watch for a Gulf polymer maker named in a designation.

  • Pushing the odds up

    Senate Banking Committee

    Holds the Iran sanctions bill introduced August 3, which would reach the shipping, insurers, and banks that serve Iranian trade. The chair sets when it’s marked up; it has 34 co-sponsors.

    Watch for the bill clearing committee; draft text from Senator Warren’s office.

  • Neutral

    National Security Council

    Wants leverage before any second round of talks, but not an oil spike before the holidays, so it leans toward hitting buyers rather than supply.

    Watch for a statement on who buys Iranian petrochemicals.

  • Neutral

    Tehran

    Wants relief without giving ground on enrichment. Boardings at sea raise the odds; agreeing to talk would lower them.

    Watch for whether it comes to the table.

  • Pulling the odds down

    European Partners

    Want the talks kept alive and oil flowing. France and Germany have asked Washington to wait for a second round of negotiation.

    Watch for a statement from the British Foreign Office.

Key Assumptions

The model’s outputs depend heavily on these assumptions. We’ve added them to this week’s watchlist.

  • HoldingBrent is at $93

    Oil stays above $85, so Washington avoids cutting supply

    If it’s wrong: a package aimed at Iran’s exports, not just its buyers, gets likelier, and the chance of sanctions would rise about 6%.

  • Under strainThe Senate text includes a 14-day window

    Any new legislation comes with a 30-day wind-down provision

    If it’s wrong: re-sourcing resin becomes impossible, and we need to act preemptively.

  • UncertainNeither has offered proof

    Our Gulf suppliers can’t quickly prove their feedstock is clean

    If it’s wrong: the resin loss in a slight expansion would fall by about half, and re-sourcing becomes less urgent.

  • HoldingNo sign of inclusion

    Gebze’s Turkish lender isn’t itself a target of sanctions

    If it’s wrong: Gebze’s payments would freeze within days, and creation of a second bank account becomes necessary.
