1Bottom Line

New sanctions are a little less likely than not. If they come, we’d have about 30 days to stop dealing with two resin suppliers, three Dubai traders, and two feeder ships. Re-sourcing Gebze’s resin and lining up a second bank pay off either way, so start both this month and hold the costlier steps until a tripwire is hit.

  1. Moderate confidence

    The Administration is more likely to widen today’s Iran sanctions than to write new ones, so buyers and shippers of Iranian petrochemicals would be hit first.

  2. High confidence

    Resin is our biggest exposure. Two Gulf suppliers that take feedstock from an Iranian-linked joint venture carry 38% of Gebze’s resin.

  3. Low confidence

    Any wind-down window is likely to be 30 days or shorter. New resin takes 6 to 8 weeks to qualify, so waiting for the announcement would be too late.

The Three Dubai Traders

They move 14% of MENA volume, some of it on to buyers we can’t see.

Staying clear of a breach

argues for pausing them now. If one is named before we act, a shipment already on the water is a breach.

Keeping MENA growing

argues for keeping them until something is announced. Rivals would take them, and they’re hard to win back.

Penumbra’s suggestion: ask all three for end-use certificates now, which costs little, and pause only a trader that won’t sign or is named.

Penumbra’s Suggested Path

  1. Now, this month

    Re-source Resin and Engage the Banks

    Qualify Singapore and Korean grades at Gebze, and open a second settlement account. $3.0M, and they protect $9.5M.

  2. At the first tripwire

    Tighten Compliance

    If the Senate schedules a floor vote, the chance passes 55%, or a Gulf polymer maker is named: certify the traders and swap the two feeders.

  3. If a sweeping package passes

    Run the Full Playbook

    Move 70% of Gebze’s resin off the Gulf, pause any trader that won’t certify, and move all Gebze payments to the new account.
