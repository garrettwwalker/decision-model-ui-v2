While the world sleeps, conditions change. You need decision intelligence that keeps up — and tells you how you can respond.

one question,
three engines.

  1. It takes the question apart.

    Real questions are tangled. Daybreak breaks each one into the parts that can actually be forecast, and tells you plainly which parts can't. No other forecaster in the field decomposes questions this way.

  2. It gives each part the right engine.

    A leader's choice, a port's traffic and a sanctions process move in different ways, so Daybreak runs three engines: one for judgment, one for telemetry, one for procedure. Each knows how far ahead it can see. Everyone else runs one kind of model on every question.

  3. It fuses the answers into one number.

    Averaging good forecasts makes a worse one. Daybreak's fusion layer combines all three engines into a single calibrated probability, with error bars. No one in the field has built that.
